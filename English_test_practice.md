## PART 2 — Grammar drills

### 2A Conditionals — rewrite (Student's Book ex 6, p.27)

Rewrite each sentence beginning with the word(s) given, keeping the meaning.

1. Could you tell her my address if, by any chance, you see her? → **Should** ____________
2. As people were dependent on farming for their livelihood, it's not surprising that they used animal behaviour to predict the weather. → **Given that** ____________
3. You can borrow my bike but you must take care of it. → **Provided that** ____________
4. Kindly have a seat as I'm sure Mr Johnson won't be long. → **If** ____________
5. My advice to you is to get another job. → **If** ____________
6. I'm not earning much money because I didn't work hard enough to pass my diploma. → **If** ____________
7. I wouldn't have been able to afford university except that my grandmother left me some money. → **But for** ____________
8. Climatic changes may, in due course, render weather lore obsolete. → **Were** ____________

### 2B Conditionals — fill the linker (ex 5, p.27)

Use: *given that · unless · as long as · provided that · but for / without · even if · on condition that · suppose / supposing*

a. ____________ lightning tends to strike the nearest high point, you would do well not to stand under a tall tree during a thunderstorm.
b. ____________ you learn to drive better, I won't be getting in your car again.
c. ____________ you use a sun screen, you shouldn't get burnt.
d. ____________ the support of my boss, I wouldn't have been promoted.
e. ____________ you do say you love me, I'm not marrying you.
f. I'll give you a lift to school ____________ you wash the car for me at the weekend.
g. ____________ there was an air traffic controllers' strike, what would you do?

### 2C Past tenses — choose the right form (ex 5, p.35)

Put the verb in brackets into a suitable tense (some need a passive **(P)** or modal **(M)**).

a. In seventeenth-century France, a nobleman's education ____________ (think: P) to be incomplete until he ____________ (master) the art of carving.
b. From the sixteenth century, women seem to ____________ (carve) meat at British tables, though by the mid-nineteenth century, carving at formal meals ____________ (carry out: P) mainly by servants.
c. The French ____________ (insist + always) that salad ____________ (tear: M+P) rather than cut with a knife, a rule which probably ____________ (arise) in order to eliminate the taste of metal.
d. Arriving in Athens pursued by the Furies because he ____________ (murder) his mother, Orestes ____________ (give: P) dinner, but so horrified were the other diners that they ____________ (eat) in silence.
e. Although live-in household staff ____________ (continue) to be the norm in America up until the 1920s, their numbers ____________ (start) to decline in Europe much earlier.
f. For the last hundred years, the separate tiers of a British wedding cake ____________ (support: P) by pillars, although more recently, upturned champagne glasses ____________ (choose: P) by some couples.

### Section D — Conditionals: key-word transformation (8 marks)
Use 3–8 words including the word given.

1. If I had known the flight would be late, I wouldn't have rushed. (**known**)
   Had I ____________ a delay to my flight, I wouldn't have rushed.
2. If you need to contact me urgently, ring this number. (**necessary**)
   Should ____________ to contact me urgently, ring this number.
3. I'm sure the hotel will be good, as Pablo recommended it. (**bound**)
   The hotel ____________ as Pablo recommended it.
4. You can stay out late only if you phone me first. (**long**)
   You can stay out late ____________ me first.

### Section E — Past tenses: open cloze (6 marks)

### Part 2A — Conditionals rewrite
1. Should you (happen to) see her, could you tell her my address?
2. Given that people were dependent on farming for their livelihood, it's not surprising that they used animal behaviour to predict the weather.
3. Provided that you take care of it, you can borrow my bike.
4. If you'd (like to) take a seat, I'm sure Mr Johnson won't be long.
5. If I were you, I'd get another job.
6. If I had worked hard enough to pass my diploma, I would be earning more money now. *(mixed conditional)*
7. But for my grandmother leaving me some money, I wouldn't have been able to afford university.
8. Were climatic changes to occur, weather lore would be rendered obsolete.

a. Given that  b. Unless  c. As long as / Provided that  d. But for / Without  e. Even if  f. on condition that  g. Suppose / Supposing
